# CropPulse Rwanda
**Know what to plant, where and when.** A free crop-planning app for all 30 Rwandan districts, with a Data Lab for agricultural statistics.

## How it works: one journey
**Select district → select season → (optional) growing conditions → ranked crops with reasons → planting window → evidence → decision brief.**
- **Plan:** *Crop Decisions*, *Seasonal Crop Plan* (provisional activity timeline), *Planting Calendar*, *Farm Planner*, and *Weather & Climate* (coordinate-based seven-day model forecast).
- **Farm workflows:** *Farm Records* (including harvest performance), *Pest & Crop Health* reports, *Extension Visits*, aggregate *Cooperative Activities*, *Recommendation Feedback*, and *Market & Economics*.
- **Evidence:** *Overview*, *Crop Analytics* (crop guide, history, seasonal trends, data brief), *District Comparison* (map + data), *Agricultural Risks*, *Data Explorer*. Every recommendation card links to its evidence.
- **About:** *Methodology & Sources* and the unauthenticated local *Agronomy Review Queue*.

## Recommendation engine (`utils/recommender.py`)
1. Validate the district, season, and any supplied elevation.
2. Show crops with a season entry in the provisional CropPulse guide; list crops without an entry separately without claiming they are locally unsuitable.
3. Apply the existing provisional elevation and water rules. Missing elevation or water information does not exclude a crop. These are not verified Rwanda thresholds.
4. Rank eligible crops by the provisional broad-zone guide category, then crop name. NISR historical observations are descriptive context only: they do not change rank, suitability, or risk.
5. Each result explains provisional fit, risks and unknowns, the unverified calendar entry, next checks, and what it cannot assess (verified local guidance, plot soil, weather-based timing, prices). Source-backed agronomic rules are managed separately from NISR statistics; the current registry has no approved rules. See [agronomic evidence review and registry workflow](docs/agronomic-evidence-review.md).
**No fit category is a probability or guarantee.** Synthetic, pending, and warning datasets cannot affect crop ranking or data-backed agronomic risk. NISR data can be shown only as historical context after local checksum and structural checks; those checks do not independently verify NISR estimates. See the [Phase 23 agricultural evidence and provenance review](docs/agricultural-evidence-provenance-phase-23.md).

Agronomic rule proposals use a separate, authenticated review workflow; internal approvals do not activate farmer guidance or verify professional qualifications. See the [Phase 24 issue disposition and rule-review workflow](docs/agronomy-rule-review-workflow-phase-24.md).

Future institutional evidence intake uses a separate versioned package store with source metadata, permission status, checksums, structural validation, and lifecycle audit events. It is local/operator-run and does not publish intake records as farmer advice. See [agronomic intake operations and limitations](data/agronomy/INTAKE.md).

## Data and honesty
Ships with synthetic demonstration data (labelled). Crop guidance (altitude, planting months, zone fit, yields) is CropPulse-compiled and **unverified by RAB/NISR**: it must be reviewed by an agronomist. To use real statistics, put a CSV and `MANIFEST.json` in `data/verified/` (see `data/templates/`); district, season, unit and checksum checks run automatically.
The Streamlit app requires the authenticated Phase 1 API before it opens dashboards; local runs default to `http://127.0.0.1:8001`, and `run_streamlit.ps1` can start or reuse that service. Production must set `CROP_PULSE_API_URL`; see [backend setup](docs/backend-phase-1.md). Farm records and saved crop plans are stored under the signed-in farmer's account. Market entries, review submissions, feedback, pest reports, extension notes, cooperative activities, and weather readings remain temporary session entries and are cleared when the user signs out. A confirmed **Clear session entries** action also removes current-session entries, not account records. Market and local weather observations are user-entered and unverified. Economics uses entered assumptions and provisional yield ranges. Pest observations do not trigger diagnoses or alerts. Cooperative logging accepts aggregate counts only and is not connected to an organization.

Weather: open **Weather & climate** in the React navigation, choose a saved field with farmer-supplied coordinates or enter farm coordinates, and request a seven-day Open-Meteo model forecast. Forecasts include provider grid coordinates, retrieval time, units, attribution, and an explicit stale warning with age; they are not observations or crop advice. Coordinates are checked against a Rwanda rectangular extent, not verified against a district boundary. The same adapter remains available in Streamlit during migration. The free provider endpoint is restricted to non-commercial use; commercial or promotional deployment requires an appropriate licence. See the [weather setup and limits](docs/weather.md).

Still not connected: verified soil data, live market prices, pest-report networks, qualified agronomic approval, managed cloud hosting, email verification, password recovery, multi-device offline support, and cross-farm sharing. Phase 1 account records are private to one farmer and depend on configured database/API services. English remains the primary language for analytics and detailed agronomy text; the Streamlit farmer workflows retain English and Kinyarwanda options. This prototype must not be treated as a production farmer service.

## React administration and farmer workspace

A React + strict TypeScript + Vite workspace is the primary farmer interface and is being expanded in migration slices alongside the existing Streamlit app. It uses the same FastAPI API and SQLite database, with cookie-based browser sessions, farmer farm/activity and crop-plan workflows, private messaging, and an authenticated point-weather forecast. React and Streamlit restore the signed-in account from its HttpOnly server cookie after a page refresh (current fixed session lifetime: 30 minutes). Both directions now support a short-lived, single-use sign-in handoff; passwords and bearer tokens are not passed in URLs, and the API-provided account role is retained across both apps. Streamlit identifies administrator and reviewer accounts as such, and its **Return to CropPulse React** link returns directly to the React overview. React, API, and Streamlit must use the same hostname for cookie-based restoration. Configure `CROP_PULSE_REACT_URL` for production; see [React app setup](docs/react-app-setup.md). Streamlit planning, analytics, and session-only tools remain available until their React replacements are implemented and checked. Hekitari remains a separate external service. For setup, first-admin provisioning, migration, backup/recovery, and release limits, see [React app setup](docs/react-app-setup.md) and [deployment checklist](docs/react-deployment-checklist.md).

The React workspace supports farmer-owned farms, optional named fields, a crop recommendation comparison, field-linked or farm-level saved plans, and plan lifecycle tracking. Recommendations come from the current FastAPI endpoint and are explicitly provisional. When a plan is saved from a selected recommendation, its context is stored with the plan; this is not an independently verified evidence record. Fields can record an optional area in hectares and farmer-supplied coordinates, which do not verify field boundaries. Farmers can also save and correct dedicated harvest records linked to their farm, optional field, and optional crop plan; yield is calculated only from validated area and quantity units and is labeled farmer-reported, not an official statistic or forecast. Each harvest now has a persistent farmer-reported post-harvest observation log for dates, handling/storage descriptions, quantities where known, condition, notes and follow-up. It does not change harvest totals or crop-plan status and does not provide storage prescriptions or spoilage diagnoses. The irrigation and water-management log records farmer-reported events with an optional source, method, farm field or crop-plan link, quantity and explicit volume unit, duration and explicit time unit, purpose, and notes. Source/method lists are defined in the UI; unknown is supported, and quantity/duration are optional. Entries do not assess water sufficiency or provide irrigation schedules or prescriptions. See [field and evidence workflow](docs/field-evidence-phase.md).

The React farm workspace also includes separate farmer-reported soil observations and soil-test records. Optional pH uses the conventional unitless 0–14 scale; measured nutrients and organic matter require explicit units. Blank measurements remain unrecorded, and the feature provides no interpretation or fertilizer advice. Lab/provider details are user-entered and not independently verified. See the [field and evidence workflow](docs/field-evidence-phase.md) and [Phase 19 implementation report](docs/soil-observations-and-tests-phase-19.md).

The farmer workspace also provides a read-only, owner-scoped [farm activity timeline](docs/farm-activity-timeline-phase-20.md) assembled from existing plans, planting dates, activities, irrigation, soil, harvest, and post-harvest records. It does not duplicate or change those records, and it does not infer missing event dates or activity.

The React workspace also includes Phase 1 private text messaging between farmers and active CropPulse reviewers assigned to a district where the farmer currently owns a farm. Administrators enable or disable it from **System health**; it is disabled by default. Reviewer role is not a qualification claim. See [private messaging setup, access rules, and limitations](docs/private-messaging-phase-1.md).

Farmers can submit private crop-health reports from **Crop health reports** in React, add an optional affected-plant-parts/spreading note, attach a validated photo, and follow reviewer updates by report reference. Maize reports also receive experimental PlantVillage model-ranked labels; scores are uncalibrated and are not diagnoses. Reports still require reviewer follow-up, and the system does not recommend pesticides or treatments. Active reviewers see cases only for currently assigned districts. See [crop-health reporting](docs/crop-health-review.md) and [model limits and setup](docs/crop-disease-model.md).

## Run (Windows PowerShell)
```
py -3.12 -m venv .venv
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pip install -r requirements.txt
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m alembic upgrade head
.\run_streamlit.ps1
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe tests\windows_safe_pytest.py
```
The local launcher starts the current FastAPI service on `127.0.0.1:8001` when needed, sets `CROP_PULSE_API_URL` for Streamlit, and stops the API it started when Streamlit exits. Before reuse, it checks API health, browser CSRF signing, CORS, and both handoff directions. Run it from PowerShell after installing dependencies and applying database migrations. For the React + API workspace, use the [Windows setup guide](docs/react-app-setup.md). React is the only password login and registration page; Streamlit redirects unauthenticated users to React and returns them to their requested workspace after sign-in. The [crop-growth workflow audit and phased backlog](docs/crop-growth-workflow-audit.md) describes connected records and remaining work.
macOS/Linux: `source .venv/bin/activate`.

Private staging has not been deployed. See the [Phase 25 staging preparation runbook](docs/private-staging-runbook-phase-25.md) for configuration, read-only migration preflight, backup/restore, smoke checks, and release blockers. The login page displays a public demonstration account; assume its credential is visible to every visitor and use it only with synthetic data.

## Structure
`knowledge.py` (crop and zone knowledge pack: add other countries here) · `plan.py` (planning pages) · `views.py` (Data Lab pages) · `utils/` (loading, validation, geography, metrics, priority) · `tests/` · `demo/` (webinar/demo recording; add the video when ready).

`product_pages.py` connects the farmer-facing workflows. `backend/api.py` provides Phase 1 authenticated farmer/farm, crop plan and farm activity storage; `backend/client.py` connects the Streamlit screens when `CROP_PULSE_API_URL` is configured. See [Backend Phase 1 setup and data boundaries](docs/backend-phase-1.md). Market, review, feedback, pest, extension, cooperative and weather entries remain session-only. The dashboard's demonstration dataset stays separate from farmer account records.

## Limits
No causal claims or yield forecasts. Weather is a seven-day model forecast at a user-supplied point, not station observation or farm-validated weather. The Rwanda bounding rectangle is only a coarse input guard. Zones and suitability are broad, curated guidance. District dots in analytics use approximate district centres. See [frontend readiness and limits](docs/frontend-readiness.md), [frontend/backend contract](docs/frontend-backend-contract.md), [competitor research](docs/competitor-research.md), and the [controlled-pilot evaluation plan](docs/pilot-evaluation.md).
